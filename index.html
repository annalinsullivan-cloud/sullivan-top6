The countdown

Six of them, worst to best

These six stand out because they are the main holidays everyone knows, each with its own clear theme, traditions, and reason to celebrate.

  1. 06

    Number six: St. Patrick's Day

    St. Patrick's Day on March 17th celebrates Irish culture with vibrant green parades, music, and a relaxed, joyful welcome to spring.

  2. 05

    Number five: Easter

    Easter celebrates Christ's resurrection alongside egg hunts, family gatherings, and a low-stress focus on spring renewal.

  3. 04

    Number four: 4th of July

    The 4th of July celebrates U.S. independence with fireworks, parades, and cookouts, earning top spots for its relaxed summer vibe and warm-weather traditions.

  4. 03

    Number three: Thanksgiving

    Thanksgiving centers on family gatherings, gratitude, and a massive feast, earning top spots for its cozy togetherness and delicious comfort food.

  5. 02

    Number two: Halloween

    Halloween on October 31st centers on costumes, trick-or-treating, and spooky fun, earning top spots for its creative self-expression, candy, and pure imagination.

  6. 01

    The best one

    Number one: Christmas

    Christmas on December 25th celebrates Christ's birth with gifts, festive decor, and cozy gatherings, earning the top spot for its magical atmosphere, deep traditions, and togetherness.

Method

How I ranked these

What I judged on

I rank holidays based on traditions, food, and time spent with family and friends. Those things make each holiday feel special and memorable.

What I ignored

Holidays can be stressful because of shopping, cooking, and family expectations, but the memories made together can make it worth it.

Where I could be wrong

Christmas can be stressful because of shopping, money, and expectations, but spending time with family makes the stress worth it.
